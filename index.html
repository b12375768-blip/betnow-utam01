Snooker or billiards — which table is yours?

The words get mixed up, so here is the short version. Both are cue sports on the same family of tables; the objective and the ball set are what differ.

Snooker

Long games, red and a colour

Twenty-two balls, pockets about 85 mm across, a 12 ft table and one ball on at a time. Red, then any colour, until the reds are gone, then the colours in sequence. Frames often run an hour or more.

  • Table: 12 ft (3569 × 1778 mm playing area)
  • Ball diameter: 52.5 mm
  • Best for: players who enjoy structure and long thinking time

Read the snooker guide

Billiards

Short games and a numbered rack

Usually called pool: English 8-ball, 9-ball and 10-ball on a 9 ft table, with 57.15 mm balls and pockets around 50 mm. Frames are quicker and combinations — one ball into another — are part of the game.

  • Table: 9 ft (2540 × 1270 mm playing area)
  • Ball diameter: 57.15 mm
  • Best for: quick sessions, groups and mixed ability

Your first visit

Most people are nervous about walking into a cue sports club for the first time. It takes about five minutes to get comfortable.

  1. Check the hours and call aheadWe are busiest on Friday and Saturday evenings. A quick call tells you which tables are free.
  2. Bring nothing but flat shoesTrainers or flat-soled shoes are fine. Cues and chalk are available to borrow at the counter.
  3. Start with an easy tableWe will point you at a table away from the league tables if you are on your own.
  4. Play a frame, then ask questionsMembers are used to beginners. Ask a referee or a coach how something works; nobody will mind.
