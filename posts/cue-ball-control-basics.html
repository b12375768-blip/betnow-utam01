Cue ball control: side, top and screw explained

Potting is only half of it. Where the white finishes decides whether the next shot exists — and that is what separates a player who pots sometimes from a player who runs frames.

Cue sports technique

7 minute readWritten by our coaching teamUpdated 1 October 2026

Spin is not a special power. It is an instruction to the cue ball about how to behave after contact. The tip tells the ball what to do; the ball obeys until friction and the cloth object to.

Contact points, simply

Where to hit the cue ball and what happens
Tip contactNameCue ball behaviour after contact
CentreStun / stopCue ball stops dead, or rolls a short distance with a slow stroke
Above centreFollowCue ball carries on in the direction it was travelling
Below centreScrew / drawCue ball spins back against its original direction, provided the stroke was firm enough
Left of centreLeft side (running)Cue ball curves slightly to the left after contact, and off the cushion
Right of centreRight side (running)Cue ball curves slightly to the right after contact, and off the cushion
Corner (high or low, off centre)Combined spinFollow or screw plus side in one shot. Powerful, and the hardest to control

Why screw so often disappoints players

Draw only works when two conditions are met: the tip must hit below centre, and the cue ball must arrive with enough speed to bite. Hit below centre with a soft stroke and the ball does not spin — it slides, follows the object ball, and goes nowhere near the place you planned. The fix is not more spin. It is more cue speed.

A useful approximation.Below-centre contact produces draw at medium to firm pace. Centre contact with a firm stroke produces a small amount of follow. Only a firm stroke with below-centre contact produces real screw.

Two side effects nobody warns you about

  • Squirt (deflection).Apply side and the tip pushes the cue ball slightly off the straight line between the balls. The effect grows with tip offset. Beginners often miss the pot by a ball width and blame the cue ball path.
  • Throw.The object ball does not travel in a perfectly straight line once it is struck off centre; spin slightly pulls or pushes it. On a slow, thin cut, throw can move the object ball a surprising distance. Aim slightly to compensate, or use less side than you think you need.

Side off the cushion

This is where side pays for itself. A cue ball with running side changes direction off a cushion; a cue ball with reverse side grips and comes off closer to a straight line. That single fact solves a huge number of position problems, because it lets you send the white to a cushion and then decide which way it returns.

  • Want the white to come back further down the table?Use running side on the side you are hitting, so it comes off at a shallower angle.
  • Want it to hug the cushion?Use reverse side and it will come off nearly straight.
  • Playing a ball off the cushion into a pocket?Running side changes the rebound angle in a way you can aim with. Practise it deliberately rather than discovering it mid-frame.

The three-ball drill

This is the exercise we give beginners who cannot control where the white finishes.

  1. Set up three balls in a lineCue ball, then an object ball 60 cm away, then a third ball placed so that the cue ball must stop at a point about 30 cm behind the second ball.
  2. Hit the middle ball centre-ballUse a firm stroke. The cue ball should stop dead. If it follows forward, your cue speed is too high; if it falls short, you are not yet firm enough.
  3. Hit below centre for drawSame line, firm stroke, tip under the centre. The cue ball should come back to about where it started. Adjust the cue height, not the power.
  4. Hit above centre for followSame line, medium stroke. The cue ball should travel forward past the second ball.
  5. Now add sideRepeat each stroke with left and right side. Work out by eye how much the starting line has to move to compensate for squirt.

Ten minutes of this twice a week does more for your scoring than a fortnight of potting the same pot.

Turning control into frames

  • Decide the finishing position before you shoot.If you cannot name the spot you want the white to stop on, you are not aiming, you are hoping.
  • Use the lowest spin that gets you there.Most position can be achieved with centre, follow or a soft draw. Side is a tool, not a default.
  • Slow the cue ball down as you get better.Slow shots give the cue ball more time to respond to side, so spin works at lower speed. Faster play is harder to control.
  • Practise the finish, not just the pot.Set up shots where the white has to stop at a marked point on the cloth. Mark it with a small piece of paper and pick it up when you finish.
