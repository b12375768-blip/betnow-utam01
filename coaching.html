Coaching, club leagues and junior training

Most improvement at the table comes from three things: a repeatable way of standing and delivering the cue, a reason to keep playing regularly, and someone who will tell you plainly what your problem is. That is what we offer.

One-to-one coaching

Sessions run for one hour on a quiet table, with the coach playing only when it helps the explanation. Bring a club member with you if you would rather watch a real frame than be the only one on the floor.

What a first session covers

  • Stance and bridge. Foot position, head still, forearm and wrist. Most supposed aiming problems are bridge problems.
  • Cue delivery. A straight, level stroke that repeats. We film it if you want to see it.
  • Potting the ball nearest you. Contact point, tip alignment and how to follow through at the right speed.
  • Where the cue ball goes afterwards. Follow, draw and side, so the next shot exists.
  • Shot selection. When to attack and when to play safe, in both snooker and pool.
  • A plan for the next month.Two or three specific things to work on, not a list of everything.

Sessions after the first continue where the last one stopped. A written note is left at the table so you can see what you agreed.

Group sessions and beginners' courses

Group sessions put a maximum of four players at a table with one coach. It costs less per player and it works well because you can see the same problem on someone else's table.

Absolute beginners

Six weekly sessions, ninety minutes each. Starts with the table, the cue and the scoreboard, then potting, then cue ball control. No previous experience assumed.

Improvers

An eight-week course for players who can pot but get stuck. Each session takes one theme: safety play, break shots, position, match routine.

Club players

Ad-hoc group sessions before a league match, or a frame-by-frame review of your own match recording. Useful for players who already compete.

Junior coaching

Sessions for younger players are grouped by age and ability, with a maximum of four juniors per table and one coach per group.

How the sessions are structured

  • Warm-up and cue control drills scaled to the group.
  • Potting technique and aiming practice on simple balls.
  • Cue ball control and how to think about the shot after contact.
  • Table manners: waiting turn, chalking, not talking over a shot.
  • A short match format so the session ends with something played, not just drills.

Groups are usually divided into under-10s, 11–13s and 14–17s. If a player is new, we start with an assessment rather than putting them straight into a match group.

For parents and guardians

  • You are welcome to stay in the room and watch.
  • Please arrive for the first session so we can go through consent, emergency contacts and anything that affects your child's comfort at the table.
  • Junior league matches are played in age bands; a younger player may be asked to move up a band once they are ready, and never because of results alone.
  • We do not use junior players in any promotional material without written permission.

Club leagues

Four leagues run through the year, all on club evenings. Entry is open to members and, for the beginners' ladder, to non-members playing at the club.

How the ladder works

  • Handicaps are set from a short assessment at the start of the season and reviewed once in the middle of it.
  • Positions are ranked on frames won, with extra weighting for a frame scored against a higher-placed opponent.
  • Promotion and relegation happen at the end of the season. There is no mid-season change based on a single result.
  • A referee is present for league frames and their ruling is final for that frame.

What a league places you in line for

  • League titles and a place on the club rankings board.
  • Priority table time during busy evenings.
  • Recognition certificates at the end-of-season presentation.
  • Selection for any club match days against other venues.
No money changes hands. Our leagues decide titles, ranking places and table time only. There are no cash prizes, no entry stakes, no side bets, and wagering money on a game of skill is not permitted on our tables. We will ask you to stop if a game starts to look like that.

League entry details are posted on the notice board and confirmed by email to players on the mailing list. Use the form below to be added to it.

Table hire and practice time

If you are not ready for a league, book table time and play. Hours are charged per table, and we will tell you the current rate at the counter.

Open table time

Tables are played in rotation on a first-come basis when they are not booked. It is the cheapest way to get comfortable at the table.

Booked tables

Reserve a specific table for an hour or longer. Booked tables are held for fifteen minutes past the start time before they are released.

Bring a partner

One person on their own can play solo practice drills. If you want a proper game, ask at the counter for a list of members playing that evening.

Coaching questions

If your question is not answered here, ask it through the enquiry form and we will add the answer to this page.

I have never held a cue. Is that a problem?

No. Half the beginners' ladder has never played before. Arrive fifteen minutes early for your first session and we will show you how the cue, chalk and scoreboard work before you hit anything.

I only want snooker, not pool. Is that fine?

Yes. We coach and compete in snooker as the main game, with pool as the quicker alternative. Tell us which you want and you will only be scheduled on those tables.

Do I need my own cue?

No for a first session: borrow one of ours and get used to the bridge and the weight. When you buy your own, the equipment guide explains what actually matters.

Can I book a coach for a group of friends?

Yes. Up to four players per table keeps the coaching useful. Send the enquiry with the number of players and your preferred evening.
